03 / ENGINEERING CASE STUDY

From manual workflows to an operational management system.

A closer look at the approach, technical architecture, and business outcomes behind the Rainier project. Descriptions are generalized to protect internal information.

THE PROBLEM

Fragmented workflows and repetitive reporting

Operational information arrived through separate exports and spreadsheets. Preparing daily rosters, reconciling performance metrics, and assembling weekly management reports required extensive manual effort.

THE SOLUTION

A connected set of applications and reporting workflows

I defined the business requirements, built an initial Excel VBA roster tool, and subsequently rebuilt that workflow as a Microsoft Access application. I developed import and reporting automation using Access, PowerShell, and Excel-based KPI logic, and integrated the work into a functional internal operations platform.

System architecture and workflow

Operational exportsRoute, attendance, payroll, and weekly reports
→
Import & validationPowerShell workflows and source checks
→

Analytics & decisionsKPI logic, reports, financial models
MY ROLE

End-to-end problem ownership

I chose how 30–50 operational KPIs should be represented, designed management reporting, defined requirements, directed AI-assisted implementation where useful, and tested and debugged results against real workflows.

MEASURABLE IMPACT

Less manual preparation, more decision support

Daily roster preparation fell from 60–90 minutes of manual work to under 3 minutes. My own estimated weekly reporting effort fell from 25–40 hours to roughly 30–60 minutes. These are reported workflow estimates, not independently audited payroll savings.
